What these network terms mean
RSRP Signal strength

The power of the 4G/5G signal reaching the router. Less negative is better: around −80 dBm is strong, −95 dBm is usable, and below −110 dBm is weak.

RSRQ Signal quality

How clean the signal is after congestion and interference. Closer to zero is better: −10 dB is good; below roughly −15 dB can be poor.

SINR Clarity

The useful signal compared with noise. Above 10 dB is good, 0–10 dB is fair, and below 0 dB usually means heavy interference.

RSSI Total received power

A broad strength reading that includes noise and interference. It is supporting context; RSRP, RSRQ, and SINR are more useful for judging mobile data quality.

Band & frequency Radio channel

The part of the mobile spectrum in use, such as 5G n78. Higher or lower is not automatically better; coverage and local capacity matter more.

Bandwidth Channel width

How much radio spectrum is allocated. Wider channels can carry more data, but actual speed still depends on signal quality, congestion, and the network.

These are practical guide ranges, not pass/fail guarantees. Different router firmware may omit some readings.
